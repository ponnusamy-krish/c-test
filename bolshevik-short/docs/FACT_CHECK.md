# Fact-check log

Every narrated claim, date label, map label and archival caption in the film, with the
sources used to check it. Dates are **O.S.** (Julian / old Russian calendar) unless marked **N.S.**
(Gregorian). In 1917 the difference was 13 days. Russia adopted the Gregorian calendar in February 1918.

## Narration

| # | Claim in the film | Verdict | Support / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Russia was one of the world's largest empires." | ✔ | Standard reference fact (largest contiguous empire of its time). Supplied by the brief. |
| 2 | "In 1917, a revolutionary party seized its capital in hours." | ✔ with nuance | Supplied by the brief. Red Guards occupied strategic points overnight 24–25 Oct; Lenin announced the government's fall on the morning of 25 Oct; ministers still in the Winter Palace were arrested in the early hours of 26 Oct [RFE-1][RFE-2][REED]. "Hours" is accurate for the seizure of the *capital* (about a day, little resistance). The film never says the whole country fell. |
| 3 | "…taking control of Russia itself was a much longer struggle." | ✔ | Civil war 1917/18–1922 [HIST][BRIT-FER]. |
| 4 | "World War One drained the empire. Millions died, and bread ran short." | ✔ with nuance | Food shortages and economic hardship [HIST]; bread protests started the February Revolution [HIST]. Deaths: Britannica gives 1.7 M Russian military dead [BRIT-WWI]; another count 1.81 M; UK National Archives "about two million" [TNA]; History.com cites about 1.5 M civilian deaths. "Millions died" is correct if civilians are included; military deaths alone are "nearly two million". Kept as narrated. |
| 5 | "In February 1917, strikes and mutinies in Petrograd forced Nicholas the Second to abdicate." | ✔ | Revolution began 23 Feb O.S. = 8 Mar N.S. with bread/strike protests [HIST]; garrison troops mutinied; Nicholas abdicated 2 Mar O.S. = 15 Mar N.S. [LOC-G][HIST]. Calendar is shown on screen to avoid a "February vs March" contradiction. |
| 6 | "A Provisional Government took over." | ✔ | [TNA][HIST]. Sources differ on the exact start (Duma committee formed 27 Feb O.S.; ministry announced 1–2 Mar O.S.), so the film says only "March 1917" for it. |
| 7 | "It kept fighting the war." | ✔ | The Provisional Government continued the war and prepared a new offensive [RFE-1]. |
| 8 | "Lenin's Bolsheviks promised peace, land, and bread, and workers and soldiers listened." | ✔ | The slogan "peace, land and bread" and the growing support among soldiers, sailors and workers in summer 1917 [RFE-1]. The Seattle Star headline of 8 Nov 1917 shows the contemporary press reading the Bolshevik programme as "quick peace and division of lands". |
| 9 | "On October twenty-fifth, by the old Russian calendar, Bolsheviks seized Petrograd's key points and toppled the Provisional Government." | ✔ | 25 Oct O.S. = 7 Nov N.S. [HIST][TNA][RFE-2]. Seizure of stations, bridges, telegraph/post office, telephone exchange and State Bank on 24–25 Oct [REED][TROTSKY][STALIN][SOV-HIST]. The State Bank was not fully under control at once (Reed): the film says only that key points were seized. |
| 10 | "November seventh, in the Gregorian calendar." | ✔ | Same sources. A US newspaper dated Thursday 8 Nov 1917 (N.S.) is shown: "ALEX KERENSKY OVERTHROWN". |
| 11 | "But holding the capital was not holding Russia." | ✔ | Bolshevik support was weak outside the industrial centres; civil war followed [RFE-2][HIST]. |
| 12 | "Civil war split the empire between Reds and Whites." | ✔ | [HIST][BRIT-FER]. |
| 13 | "By 1922, through war and tight centralised control, the Bolsheviks had won." | ✔ | Red Army entered Vladivostok and the Far Eastern Republic was absorbed in late 1922 [BRIT-FER]; USSR founded 30 Dec 1922 [BRIT-M][LOC-B]. Note: History.com says the civil war "ended in 1923". That is not supported by the other sources (Far East 1922) and was not used. Historians date the end variously (Nov 1920 in Crimea [RFE-3], 1921, Oct 1922). |
| 14 | "The capital fell in hours. Consolidating power took years." | ✔ | Supplied by the brief; same support as #2 and #13. |

## Images: what is genuine and what is not

* Every photograph and newspaper page is an authentic archival item with its date and rights recorded in `assets/manifest.json` and `docs/SOURCES.md`.
* No photograph was generated, retouched beyond a monochrome grade, or presented as something it is not.
* Items **rejected** on verification: see `docs/ASSET_NOTES.md` (film stills presented as "the storming of the Winter Palace", paintings of Lenin, a caption that LoC itself flags as unverified, images with doubtful rights).
* Graphics (maps, arrows, territory shading, date ribbon, titles) are illustrative and drawn by the production code; they are never presented as archival footage.
